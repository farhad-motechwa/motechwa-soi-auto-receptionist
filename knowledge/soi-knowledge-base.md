## About the Institute

The Sayyidina Omar Institute is a youth character-formation and mentorship hub — "Raising
Muslims of Character" — helping young Muslims grow into people of taqwa (God-consciousness),
courage, justice, and service, inspired by the legacy of Sayyidina Umar ibn al-Khattab RA.
It is not a school, university, or tuition centre. Its work spans structured programmes,
mentor formation, youth innovation projects, free daily-practice apps, and media/community
broadcasting.

## How You See Yourself at 30 — flagship youth formation programme

A four-book mentorship series plus an in-person programme for high school students aged
roughly 15 to 18, delivered in small-group "Tarbiyah Circles" with trained Murabbis —
separate, equally-resourced circles for young men and young women.

Four pedagogical foundations:
- **Tadabbur and Tafakkur** — guided Qur'anic reflection and journaling.
- **Tarbiyah Circles** — small-group honest conversation and mutual reminder.
- **Accountable Suhbah** — companionship with adult Murabbis who model rather than lecture.
- **Basirah** (spiritual sight) — seeing beyond grades and social status toward eternal purpose.

The programme uses a shared reflective vocabulary called the **Sovereign Compass**, covering
four inner dimensions: Qalb (the heart — moral orientation and intention), 'Aql (the
intellect — reasoning and discernment), Nafs (the ego — appetite and status anxiety), and Ruh
(the spirit — remembrance and trust in Allah). This is an educational vocabulary for
self-reflection, not a clinical or diagnostic tool.

Its personal output is the **"30-Year Vision Seed"**: a five-field reflection covering
relationship with Allah, character and inner state, family responsibility, knowledge and
livelihood, and service and contribution.

The curriculum follows the **Lunar Centric Curriculum** — it teaches the module that matches
the Islamic lunar season the students are living through, using the school term only as the
delivery container, not the driver of pacing. Five modules:
- **Module A — "The Awakening"** (Sha'ban and pre-Ramadan): emptying out and preparing for Ramadan.
- **Module B — "The Crucible"** (Ramadan itself): fasting, Qur'anic immersion, night worship.
- **Module C — "The Consolidation"** (Shawwal and Dhul-Qa'dah): protecting what was gained and building steady routines.
- **Module D — "The Return"** (Dhul-Hijjah): sacrifice, mortality, and true success, inspired by Sayyidina Ibrahim AS.
- **Module E — "The Foundations"** (Muharram to Rajab): the core nine-week sequence covering theology, character, adab, and the Vision Seed.

## Murabbi Formation Programme — adult mentor pathway

Trains and holds accountable the adult volunteers ("Murabbis") who accompany young people
across the Institute's programmes. A Murabbi is a trusted adult guide, not a lecturer, life
coach, or motivational speaker — someone who models character (adab), listens deeply, and
accompanies young people with care and theological restraint, without issuing independent
religious rulings.

Formation covers safeguarding and boundaries (including a Working With Children Check),
active listening and empathy, and knowing the limits of the Murabbi role — referring anything
involving mental health or serious distress to appropriate professionals rather than handling
it themselves.

Anyone wanting to volunteer or ask about becoming a Murabbi should have an enquiry logged
under category "Murabbi Formation" — the receptionist does not assess suitability.

The Murabbi Formation Programme runs weekly: an online session on Wednesday evenings from
8:15 to 9:45 pm on Google Meet, and an in-person session on Saturday mornings from 9 to 10 am
at Alhidayah Centre. Course material is on the learning portal. Intake dates, fees and how
places are confirmed are not published here, so log an enquiry under category "Murabbi
Formation" rather than stating them. If asked when the next intake is, say the team will
confirm it.

## Project Amanah — youth innovation pilot

A practical, project-based programme for high-school and university-aged youth in Perth. It
teaches a four-step method: find a real community problem, understand the system behind it,
use AI and technology with discernment (never as an authority on truth or ethics), and build
a responsible, ethical response. Piloting from 2026 to 2027 as a workshop series. Exact
dates, venue confirmation, and eligibility beyond "high school and university youth in Perth"
are not published — log an enquiry under category "Project Amanah".

## Online workshops and seminars

teachers, madrasah volunteers and mentors who want a reflective pause before offering guidance
to young people. Each session is a stand-alone evening and is run on Google Meet from 8:15 to
